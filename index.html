01 / THE MISSION

Distance should never be the barrier to care.

Project Aether explores how engineering and drone technology could make essential medical supplies more reachable for people living far from hospitals and established healthcare facilities.

The central idea is simple: rethink the last mile of medical delivery, especially when roads, terrain, and distance make ordinary transportation difficult.

03 / THE CONCEPT
THE PROJECT AETHER APPROACH

A delivery system
built around the payload.

04 / PAYLOAD & TEMPERATURE

Delivery is only half
the challenge.

Medical supplies can have different storage requirements. Project Aether considers how a payload compartment could help maintain appropriate conditions during transport.

DESIGN PRIORITY

Protect the payload.

A future version of the system would need to be designed and tested against the handling requirements of each specific payload.

▣

Temperature-sensitive medicines

Some medicines require a controlled temperature range. The correct range depends on the specific medicine and its manufacturer guidance.

✚

Blood products

Blood components have specific storage and transport requirements. A dedicated, validated transport solution would be essential.

⌁

Monitoring & insulation

Potential design elements include insulation, temperature sensing, and a way to record conditions during a delivery.

Important: This is a design concept, not a certified medical transport system. No medicine or blood product should be transported using an unvalidated prototype.

06 / THE PERSON BEHIND THE PROJECT
A PERSONAL MOTIVATION

This project
is personal.

HASAN AYUB · PROJECT FOUNDER

My name is Hasan Ayub. I am a student at West Windsor-Plainsboro High School North in New Jersey, and I started Project Aether because I wanted to work on a problem that mattered beyond the project itself.

When my grandfather passed away from a rare pulmonary cancer in 2019, it deeply affected me. I remember wanting to find a way to help and wishing that I could make a difference for families facing serious illness.

I was also born in Pakistan, where regions such as Khyber Pakhtunkhwa (KPK) include remote, mountainous communities. For people living far from medical facilities, access to care and supplies can be difficult.

Project Aether brings those motivations together. It is my attempt to use engineering to explore a practical way of improving access to healthcare.

“I want Project Aether to help all who don’t have access to hospitals and medicine.”
